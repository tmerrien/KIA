### 1.1 Gricean and neo-Gricean pragmatics (track A)

| Person(s) | Work (note) | Main idea used by this crawl |
|---|---|---|
| H. P. Grice | *Logic and Conversation* 1975 (`A-pragmatics/grice-1975-logic-conversation.md`) | Cooperative Principle. Quantity has two sides: as informative as required, and no more. Manner's "be brief" covers only *unnecessary* prolixity. Quality outranks Manner. Excess content makes hearers infer it has a "POINT". Only pp. 45-47 read. |
| L. R. Horn | Q- and R-based implicature, 1984 (`A-pragmatics/horn-1984-q-r-implicature.md`) | Q: say as much as you can. R: say no more than you must. Division of pragmatic labour: an unmarked short form gets the stereotype, and a marked long form signals a non-stereotypical meaning. Primary NOT read; quotations are second-hand. |
| S. C. Levinson | *Presumptive Meanings* 2000 (`A-pragmatics/levinson-2000-presumptive-meanings-qim.md`) | Q/I/M heuristics with ranking Q > M > I. Articulation is the bottleneck (under 100 baud), so "say little and infer much", but only where the inference is reliable and fast. "Not unreliable" is read as less than "reliable". Book NOT read; 1995 chapter read. |
| R. van Rooij, M. Franke | SEP entry on OT and game-theoretic implicature, rev. 2026 (`A-pragmatics/vanrooij-franke-2026-biot-games-horn-division.md`) | Derives Horn's division from Blutner's bidirectional OT and from signalling-game equilibria. Needs a cost ranking on forms and a probability ranking on meanings. An anti-Horn equilibrium is also stable. |
| L. Bergen, R. Levy, N. Goodman | Lexical uncertainty, 2016 (`A-pragmatics/bergen-levy-goodman-2016-lexical-uncertainty.md`) | Baseline RSA cannot derive M-implicature. Lexical uncertainty ties the costly form to the rare meaning. A short form is penalised when it is *vague*, not when it is short. Ignorance is a marked state. |
| D. Wilson, D. Sperber | Relevance Theory, 2004 (`A-pragmatics/wilson-sperber-2004-relevance-theory.md`) | Relevance is effect against effort. "Chicken" beats "meat" on effect and beats the equivalent disjunction on effort. Make the first accessible reading the intended one. Specific figures are read as exhaustive. Rounding is fine unless it loses effects. |

### 1.3 Clark school: common ground, grounding, overhearers (track A)

| Person(s) | Work (note) | Main idea |
|---|---|---|
| H. H. Clark, S. Haviland | Given-new contract 1977; "What's new?" 1974 (`A-pragmatics/clark-haviland-1977-given-new-contract.md`) | Given information addresses a unique antecedent in memory. A missing antecedent forces bridging (+181 ms). Uniqueness, appropriateness and computability must not be violated. Given before new is a processing argument. |
| H. H. Clark, D. Wilkes-Gibbs | Collaborative reference, 1986 (`A-pragmatics/clark-wilkesgibbs-1986-collaborative-reference.md`) | Least *collaborative* effort. Descriptions shrank from 41 words to 8 once accepted ("the ice skater"). Shortening works by simplifying, not truncating. Writers carry "distant responsibility". |
| M. Schober, H. H. Clark | Addressees vs overhearers, 1989 (`A-pragmatics/schober-clark-1989-addressees-overhearers.md`) | Accuracy: matchers 99%, early overhearers 88%, late overhearers 68%. A pause button did not help. Coined labels are precise for participants and compressed for everyone else. |
| S. Brennan, H. H. Clark | Conceptual pacts, 1996 (`A-pragmatics/brennan-clark-1996-conceptual-pacts.md`) | An accepted term becomes a partner-specific pact and is kept even when over-specific. Pacts shorten within one perspective. Hedges disappear once a term is ratified. Pacts reset for a new partner. Synonym switching signals a new perspective. |
| J. E. Fox Tree | Monologues vs dialogues, 1999 (`A-pragmatics/foxtree-1999-overhearing-monologues-dialogues.md`) | Overhearers understood dialogues better (1.57 vs 2.27 errors) but preferred monologues. The dialogue advantage tracks discourse markers (r = .73, n = 8) and multiple perspectives. Fox Tree & Mayer 2008 attribute it to perspectives. |

### 1.4 Information theory and psycholinguistic efficiency (track B, plus Gibson 1998 in C)

| Person(s) | Work (note) | Main idea |
|---|---|---|
| C. E. Shannon | 1948 (`B-information-theory/shannon-1948-source-channel-redundancy.md`) | Source coding removes redundancy. Channel coding adds it back, but only "in the proper way" for the particular noise. English is about 50% redundant. Basic English expands text while exact vocabulary condenses it. Fidelity is set by the receiver. |
| C. E. Shannon | 1951 (`B-information-theory/shannon-1951-prediction-entropy-printed-english.md`) | About 1 bit per letter with 100 letters of context (about 75% redundancy). "Reduced text" is lossless only for a "mathematically identical twin". Information concentrates at branch points. Scientific text is less predictable. |
| S. Piantadosi, H. Tily, E. Gibson | Word length, *PNAS* 2011 (`B-information-theory/piantadosi-2011-wordlength.md`) | Contextual surprisal predicts word length better than frequency in 10 of 11 languages. Long but predictable collocation words buck the trend, which is the analogue of stock filler. |
| S. Piantadosi, H. Tily, E. Gibson | Ambiguity, *Cognition* 2012 (`B-information-theory/piantadosi-2012-ambiguity.md`) | An efficient code sends H[M\|C], not H[M], so it looks ambiguous out of context. This assumes a *shared* coding scheme and context. Omitting what the context does not carry is inefficient ambiguity. |
| B. Juba, A. T. Kalai, S. Khanna, M. Sudan | Compression without a common prior, 2011 (`B-information-theory/juba-2011-compression-without-common-prior.md`) | A code tuned to the speaker's prior is brittle. A robust code costs H(p) + 2 lg α. Every unambiguous code wastes bits. The speaker must make the intended meaning the most likely reading for *every* reader in the audience. |
| K. Mahowald, E. Fedorenko, S. Piantadosi, E. Gibson | Short forms in predictive contexts, 2013 (`B-information-theory/mahowald-2013-short-forms-predictive-contexts.md`) | Short form chosen 67% after a predictive context vs 56% after a neutral one. Long forms carry +1.45 bits even at matched frequency. Cai et al.: ChatGPT and Vicuna lack this sensitivity. |
| R. Levy, T. F. Jaeger | UID, 2007; Jaeger 2010 (`B-information-theory/levy-jaeger-2007-uniform-information-density.md`) | Optional "that" is kept before surprising material. Redundant structure words act as pressure valves that flatten information spikes. |
| M. Aylett, A. Turk | Smooth Signal Redundancy, 2004 (`B-information-theory/aylett-turk-2004-smooth-signal-redundancy.md`) | Acoustic redundancy varies inversely with predictability. An even profile is more robust (0.5 × 0.5 = 0.25 vs 0.1 × 0.9 = 0.09). Prominence goes to the unpredictable. 2004 text NOT read. |
| C. Meister, T. Pimentel, ..., R. Levy | Revisiting UID, EMNLP 2021 (`B-information-theory/meister-2021-revisiting-uid.md`) | Uneven surprisal lowers acceptability. The best fit is regression toward a language-wide mean, so troughs (padding) and spikes (telegraphese) both deviate. |
| E. Gibson, R. Futrell, S. Piantadosi, et al. | How efficiency shapes language, *TiCS* 2019 (`B-information-theory/gibson-2019-efficiency.md`) | Efficiency is minimal effort *subject to communicative success*. Context resolves most ambiguity. Zero-ambiguity codes (contracts, code) turn pedantic. Information and dependency locality. |
| E. Gibson | Dependency locality, 1998 (`C-controlled-language/gibson-1998-dependency-locality.md`) | Cost is driven by the number of *new discourse referents* between dependents, not by length. Given referents (I, you) add almost nothing. Heavy-last and extraposition. A new synonym counts as a new referent. |
| G. K. Zipf | 1935/1949 (not covered) | Least effort and the frequency-length law. Root of Horn's two economies and of the Piantadosi line. |

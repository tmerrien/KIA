# Morkes & Nielsen (1997): Concise, SCANNABLE, and Objective: How to Write for the Web

**Citation:** Morkes, J., & Nielsen, J. (1997, January 1). *Concise, SCANNABLE, and Objective: How to Write for the Web.* Nielsen Norman Group.
**Link:** https://www.nngroup.com/articles/concise-scannable-and-objective-how-to-write-for-the-web/ (read in full via the NN/g page)

## Core claim
Web readers scan; they do not read word by word. Text that is concise (about half the words), scannable (headings, bullets, bold keywords, one idea per paragraph, conclusion first) and objective (no hype or boasting) each measurably improves usability. The three effects stack: the combined version was 124% better than promotional control copy.

## Key evidence
- Three studies, 81 users in total (11 + 19 + 51). Study 3 used a 5-condition between-subjects design with 51 experienced web users (mean age 41). All versions were a 7-page "Travel Nebraska" site with **the same information and hypertext structure**.
- Measures: task time, task errors, memory, sitemap time and subjective satisfaction, combined by geometric mean into an Overall Usability score (control = 100).
- Results: Concise 158 (+58%), Scannable 147 (+47%), Objective 127 (+27%), Combined 224 (+124%).
- The authors call it a single, simple site and ask for replication. They also report that a later Sun rewrite using the same guidelines gave a 159% improvement. The Sun study's details are UNVERIFIED (not read).

## Relevance to AI answer style
- **Precision vs compression.** In the "Concise" condition the information content stayed fixed (the same information in every version) and only the wording was cut. This is a controlled test of shortening without losing meaning. The gain came from removing words that carried no information, not from removing facts. Compression in the bad sense (dropping meaning) was never tested and should not be credited with these gains.
- **Objective beat promotional.** Hedging, praise and self-promotion ("Great question!", "I'd be happy to help") are the chat-assistant version of marketese. Removing them was worth +27% on its own.
- **Inverted pyramid.** Putting the conclusion first maps directly onto answer-first replies.
- Caveat: the participants were web scanners doing look-up tasks. For reasoning-heavy answers the gains may differ.

## Candidate rules for the harness
1. Put the answer or conclusion in the first sentence, and the supporting detail after it (inverted pyramid).
2. Cut words that carry no information (filler, promotional or self-referential phrases, restatements of the question). Never cut a fact, condition or caveat the user needs. Hold the information constant and shrink only the wording.
3. Make longer answers scannable: one idea per paragraph, a topic sentence first, and lists only for parallel items.

## Core claim
Signaling means cues that steer attention to the relevant information or show the structure: headings, bold, colour, arrows and intonation. It reliably improves retention (g = 0.53, a medium effect) and transfer (g = 0.33, a small effect). It also lowers cognitive load slightly. The benefit depends on the kind of cue. Permanent cues inside text, such as headings and bold words, are among the strongest. Flashing cues hurt learning.

## Key evidence (all via CHU Kurzreview 22)
- **Corpus:** 103 studies, N = 12,201, published 1970-2016 in English or German. The pool covers journals, theses, conference papers and online work. It contains 139 retention comparisons and 70 transfer comparisons. Most studies were controlled lab experiments, and about 14% of participants were secondary-school pupils.
- **Main effects:** retention g = 0.53 and transfer g = 0.33. Cognitive load fell (g = 0.25), motivation rose (g = 0.13) and learning time increased (g = 0.30). Eye tracking shows longer fixation on signaled passages (g = 0.39).
- **Text-signal type, retention/transfer (k = retention/transfer):**
  - Organisational cues such as headings and bold words ("Strukturierungshilfen"): k = 57/9, g = 0.71* / 0.35*
  - Text-to-picture references: k = 5/2, 0.38 / 0.27*
  - Colour: k = 2/0, 0.70 / -
  - Intonation in speech: k = 2/2, 0.43 / 0.70*
  - Mixed: k = 3/2, 1.01* / 0.29
- **Picture-signal type:**
  - Colour: k = 18/16, 0.44* / 0.39*
  - Signposts (arrows, pedagogical agents): k = 19/12, 0.33* / 0.21
  - Labels: k = 6/4, 0.32 / 0.38
  - Spotlight: k = 7/7, 0.17 / 0.29
  - Graphic organisers: k = 4/3, 0.32 / 0.32
  - **Flashing: k = 3/3, -0.56 / -0.47**, so flashing harms transfer
  - Mixed: k = 16/12, 0.77* / 0.48*
- **Permanence:** permanent cues 0.58* / 0.31* (k = 84/27); temporary cues 0.46* / 0.35* (k = 53/38).
- **Prior knowledge:** low 0.42* / 0.39* (k = 37/22); high 0.67* / 0.55* (k = 20/16). CHU says this is evidence AGAINST an expertise-reversal pattern for signaling, which contradicts Richter et al. (2016). The CHU review also says no moderator besides cue type and subject reached significance, so the high-low difference is a trend, not an established reversal.
- **Pacing:** system-paced 0.57* / 0.38*; learner-paced 0.53* / 0.30*. The difference was not significant.
- **Age:** university students 0.60* / 0.32*; primary pupils 0.19 / 0.21 (k = 7/7, not significant).
- **Subject:** mathematics is null (0.08 / -0.03). The authors attribute this to little text and highly abstract content.
- **Publication era:** before 2000, transfer g = 0.06 (k = 9). After 2000, transfer g = 0.37* (k = 61).
- UNVERIFIED / likely transcription error: the CHU table gives identical values (0.68 / 0.48) for the "text" and "picture" representation levels. Its prose says the text effect on retention is larger than the picture effect. Check this against the primary paper before using either number.
- CHU caveat: most moderator cells rest on small k, and not every study codes every moderator, so read the moderator estimates with caution.

## Relevance to AI answer style
Headings and bold are the "organisational" text cues, and they carry the largest text-signaling effect on retention (g = 0.71, k = 57). They are justified when they mark the structure or the key content of an answer. The transfer evidence for them is thin (k = 9, g = 0.35). The negative result for flashing cues is the over-signaling warning: a cue that grabs attention without marking relevance costs learning. In text, the equivalent is bolding many phrases, which removes the contrast that makes bold work. That last step is an inference; the meta-analysis did not test bold density. The paper does NOT give a dose-response curve for how much to signal, so any density limit in a rule is a harness design choice, not a finding.

On precision versus compression: signaling adds no content words. It compresses navigation, not meaning. A heading earns its place when it names exactly what the section holds, the way a precise term does. It is filler when it is generic ("Overview", "Details").

## Candidate rules for the harness
1. **Use structure cues (headings, bold) only to mark relevance or organisation.** Each bolded phrase must be one of the few things the reader must not miss. Grounds: organisational text cues g = 0.71 retention. Flashing, salient but irrelevant cues are negative.
2. **Prefer permanent, in-text cues over decorative ones.** Write descriptive headings and bold the key terms. Do not rely on emoji, colour or ornament, which have little or no support in the text-cue data (colour k = 2).
3. **Do not drop signaling for expert users by default.** This meta-analysis found no expertise reversal for signaling: the high-prior-knowledge effect was numerically larger. Cut cues only because the answer is short (a three-line answer needs no headings), not because the reader is an expert. The second half of this is an inference from the data; the first rests on the moderator trend.
